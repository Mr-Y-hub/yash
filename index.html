I spent seventeen months at FIS Global, a Fortune 500 financial technology firm, owning the vulnerability management lifecycle across a global, multi-site estate and working SIEM triage alongside it. The environment was regulated, the kit was mixed, and a meaningful share of it could not simply be patched on a Tuesday.

That constraint is where most of what I know came from. Anyone can produce a scan report. The work is deciding what actually matters when the exploit intelligence shifts, negotiating a compensating control for a legacy AIX host inside a change freeze, and writing the residual risk up clearly enough that a director accepts it knowingly rather than by default.

I am now completing an MSc in Cybersecurity at King's College London, working part-time alongside it, and keeping offensive practice current — top 1% globally on TryHackMe across penetration testing, web exploitation and reverse engineering. I hold CompTIA Security+, write Python comfortably, and have built and shipped small tools on LangChain and LLM APIs.

Summary of assurance: four engagements assessed at section 3.0, all evidenced by direct participation. Tooling depth is rated honestly at section 4.0 — production experience is distinguished from lab practice rather than presented as equivalent.

This document covers professional security work from January 2022 to the present, across four organisations: one enterprise financial services employer, two internships, and one volunteer open-source engagement. Bar and events work held alongside postgraduate study is out of scope here and appears on the full CV.

Each entry at section 3.0 records the environment, the period, what I was responsible for, and what came out of it. Where a claim is measurable I have given the number; where it is not, I have said what I did rather than inflating it into a metric.

Rating scale used throughout section 4.0 — Production: used routinely in a live enterprise environment with real consequences for error. Working: used to complete real assessments, projects or engagements. Lab: practised in controlled environments and CTFs, not yet run in production. Ratings are self-assessed and stated conservatively.

CAP-001

Enterprise vulnerability management & security operations

Verified
Organisation
FIS Global — IT Security Analyst
Period
May 2024 – Sept 2025 · 17 months
Environment
Global multi-site financial services, remote
Frameworks in scope
NIST CSF · ISO 27001 · HIPAA · PCI-DSS

Responsibility

Owned the vulnerability lifecycle end to end across the estate, and shared the SIEM triage rota alongside it.

Work performed

  • Ran scheduled and ad-hoc scanning in Rapid7 InsightVM and Qualys, hand-validating output to strip false positives before anything reached an asset owner.
  • Maintained a standing backlog of roughly 500–1,000 open findings, re-prioritising as fresh scan data arrived and as exploit intelligence changed the picture on items previously parked.
  • Handled network-layer findings across Cisco switching and routing, IBM AIX hosts and F5 BIG-IP appliances. Where patching was not viable — legacy kit, change freezes, vendor dependencies — designed compensating controls and documented the residual risk for formal acceptance.
  • Mapped findings to the control frameworks the business was audited against. Telling a stakeholder which control they were failing, not merely which CVE they held, was usually what unblocked remediation.
  • Drove remediation with business units and patching teams: scheduled check-ins, escalation on SLA breach, and verification scanning afterwards to confirm the fix had actually landed.
  • Triaged 10–50 Splunk alerts a week, correlating across sources, closing noise, and escalating genuine incidents with sufficient context that the next responder did not restart the investigation.
  • Investigated anomalies with no matching playbook and no clean signature, working from evidence back to root cause without taking production services down to do it.

Outcome

  • Mean time to remediate fell across the programmes tracked.
  • Authored the reporting line to senior stakeholders — translating findings such as an unauthenticated RCE on an internet-facing appliance into a decision a non-technical director could act on.
  • Became the informal first point of contact for security questions from teams outside IT: suspected phishing, software safety, and what a given policy meant in practice.
